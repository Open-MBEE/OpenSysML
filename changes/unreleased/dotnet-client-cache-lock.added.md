- The .NET client takes the shared `~/.opensysml/bin` cache's lock exactly as the Python, Java and Rust clients take it, and starts the cached `sysml-grpc` from a copy hard linked under its own digest, as they do, so a release another client installs over the cache afterwards never changes what is running. The lock is held with `lockf` on a descriptor libc opened (POSIX) or `LockFileEx` over the whole file (Windows), behind a `.dotnet` guard file that shuts out other copies of the client loaded into the same process; waits are cancellable, back off to a quarter second, warn after two seconds and give up after two minutes, and the kernel releases everything when a holder ends, however it ends. Releases are installed through staged files that are verified before an atomic rename, the executable before its record, and a record vouches for the executable only while its digest matches, so no interruption leaves a torn pair trusted. Where the cache's file system cannot hard link, the executable is copied with a warning; where the cache cannot be locked or linked at all, it is started in place with a warning. Tests prove exclusion both ways against holders making each other client's exact calls, between processes and between collectible load contexts, and recovery from a process killed at every step of a transaction; CI requires them on Linux, macOS and Windows.
- The .NET cache rebuilds symbolic-link snapshots, reports malformed Unicode metadata as unusable, and checks cancellation immediately before executable publication. Foreign lock-holder tests stop and await their processes when acquisition fails.
