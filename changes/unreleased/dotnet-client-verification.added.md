- The .NET client verifies and analyzes models. `Model.VerifyConstraintAsync` and `VerifyRequirementAsync` answer a `Verification` whose `Verdict` carries the condition that failed, the question asked and its status, the solver's `Witness` assignment and the `VerdictNote`s its answer rests on, the answering engine's `Standing` and the objects it is about, with the verification cases verifying a requirement beside it; `VerifyOptions` names the question (`evaluate`, `holds`, `satisfiable`), a subject, positional and named arguments and an engine. `VerifySatisfactionAsync` and `SatisfiedAsync` answer every satisfaction assertion, and `ValidateInstanceAsync` every assertion about an object and the objects it holds, with a summary verdict. `CalcAsync` invokes a calculation with positional arguments or evaluates a calc usage's outputs; `RunAnalysisAsync` runs an analysis case with its subject, arguments, schedule and engine, reporting outputs, objective and assertion verdicts, verification verdicts, trade-study evaluations and a tool dry run's preview, and `ExploreAnalysisAsync` explores it, the `Exploration` now carrying the engine's `Standing`; `RunSweepAsync` runs a case or calc once per row of stepped or seeded sampled `SweepRange`s; and `Connection.ListEnginesAsync` lists the analysis engines, probing external ones only when asked. A false or undecided verdict is an answer, never an exception, and a failed sweep row stays a row; a request the service could not answer raises `VerificationException` with its `FailureReason`, and a failed analysis `AnalysisException` keeping the partial run. The conformance runner covers these eight methods, and its comparison with the reference runner leaves out the wall time a sweep row reports.
