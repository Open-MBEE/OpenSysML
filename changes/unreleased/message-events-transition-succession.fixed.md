- **A `message` owns its ends as `EventOccurrenceUsage`s, and a `transition` owns its `then` succession.** The RDF and API-JSON export wrote a `message m from a to b;` end as an unnamed `ReferenceUsage` under an `EndFeatureMembership` — the connector-end shape, so a `message` read back as `flow` — and a `TransitionUsage` carried only `sysml:source`/`sysml:target` with no `SuccessionAsUsage`. A message's ends are now `sysml:EventOccurrenceUsage`s owned through `sysml:ParameterMembership`s (SysML.xtext `MessageEventMember`), and a transition owns its `then` edge as a `sysml:SuccessionAsUsage` through a `sysml:OwningMembership` (SysML.xtext `TransitionSuccessionMember`), stated as `sysml:succession` — the empty source end plus the connector end whose `ReferenceSubsetting` names the target. `flow` ends keep the connector shape, a `message` reads back as `message` from the graph alone — its ends included when the graph states their `ParameterMembership`s only from the membership's side (`sysml:membershipOwningNamespace` or `sysml:owningRelatedElement`), with no `sysml:ownedMembership` on the flow — the owned succession is implied rather than written back as a member, and a succession whose ends disagree with the transition's `sysml:source`/`sysml:target` is refused. When a graph links only some of a message's ends from the flow itself and the rest from the memberships' side, `sysx:memberIndex` or the flow's `sysml:sourceFeature`/`sysml:targetFeature` orders them — a message neither source orders is refused rather than written with its ends reversed.
