- The .NET client edits models. `Model.Edit()` starts an `Editor` that collects edits in call order, with a method for each kind and the action-body sequencing shorthands, and applies them as one batch with `ApplyAsync`; `Model.ApplyEditsAsync` applies any list of `Edit`s. An `Edit` is an immutable record of one of the service's seventeen kinds (`SetValue`, `Rename`, `AddMember`, `Delete`, `Move`, `AddConnection`, `AddSatisfy`, `AddRequirementConstraint`, `AddTransition`, `AddImport`, `AddDocumentation`, `AddVerify`, `AddMetadata`, `AddSequence`, `AddMetadataPrefix`, `AddComment`, `AddNote`) with every option it has, an `AddSequence` nesting the `Body` and `ElseBody` of its statements, which `Editor.Body` collects. The answer is an `EditResult` of the edited notation, each `EditedDocument` the edits rewrote and each `AppliedEdit`; a model of several documents is edited as one, a document named by `EditOptions.InDocument`, and its notation is in `Documents` alone. A refused batch raises `EditException` with its `EditFailure` kind, diagnostics and `Referrer`s. The capabilities a batch needs are checked over the whole batch, in the service's order, before anything is sent. `EditResult.SaveAsync` and `EditedDocument.SaveAsync` write the edited notation without ever leaving a partial file or replacing one unless asked. The conformance runner covers ApplyEdits.
