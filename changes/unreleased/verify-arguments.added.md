- **A constraint or requirement with `in` parameters is verified with arguments for them.** `VerifyConstraint` and `VerifyRequirement` take `arguments`, bound positionally in declaration order, and `named_arguments`, bound by name, as `RunAnalysis` binds a case's inputs; a name no parameter has, more positional values than parameters, a parameter left without a value, default or same-named value on the checked object, or a value not of the parameter's type is an undecided verdict naming it, and the symbolic `holds` and `satisfiable` questions refuse them. The service advertises the `verification_arguments` capability; every client binds them (`VerifyArguments`/`VerifyArgument` in Go, `arguments=`/`named_arguments=` in Python, Julia and MATLAB, `VerifyOptions` in Node, Rust and Java). `%constraint`, `%requirement`, `-constraint` and `-requirement` take them in parentheses after the name, with an object as the subject after them, as `%analysis` does: `%requirement Pkg::Under(limit = 5) Pkg::part`.
