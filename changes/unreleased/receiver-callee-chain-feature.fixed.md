- A call that reaches its function through a chain of names (`q.p.f(1)`) wrote the receiver `q.p` as an expression at `sysml:operand` without owning it — an element with no owner — and the reflective read had nothing to mirror. The conversion now writes the form KerMLExpressions gives it (`InstantiatedTypeMember` → `OwnedFeatureChain`): an `OwningMembership` owning a chain `Feature` with one `FeatureChaining` per link, `sysml:function` naming the last, the arguments alone as `in` parameters; it reads the chain's links back as the segments they are, and the reflective read owns the same Feature, chainings and ids (`ExpressionChainFeature`, `ChainingFeatures`). A receiver that is not a pure chain of names (`(a + b).f(1)`) keeps its operand form; `p.a.b` stays one `FeatureChainExpression` per link.
