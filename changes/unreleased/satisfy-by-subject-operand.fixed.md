- The `by` operand of a satisfy usage is a feature reference or feature chain (SysML.xtext `SatisfactionReferenceExpression`), and one written as a wider expression (`satisfy requirement sr : R1 by sub#(1);`, `by (a, b)`, `by parts->first()`, `by self.items#(2)`, `by new Sub()`) is now reported once, where the chain ends, with the usage closing at its `;` so the members after it parse undisturbed, instead of a cascade of three diagnostics about the declaration, a metadata prefix and a body member; a `by` subject the next line's member follows is still reported as the missing `;` it is, while an operator opening the next line (`by a` / `+ b;`) extends the operand and is reported once as the expression it makes.
- A satisfy usage declared without `by` inside a part or part definition (`part craft : Lander { satisfy requirement touchdown : Touchdown; }`) takes the owning object as the requirement's subject (SysML v2 §7.21.4): reading its `result` (`craft.touchdown.result`, `Requirements.sysml` `RequirementCheck::result`) now evaluates the requirement's conditions on that object and agrees with `-satisfy`, and reading the subject itself (`craft.touchdown.lander.verticalSpeed`) answers about that object, where both read `<undetermined>`; an owner the subject's type or multiplicity refuses leaves the subject unbound, and the `result` read reports the refusal as the check does.
