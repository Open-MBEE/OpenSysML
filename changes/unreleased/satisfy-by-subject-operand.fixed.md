- The `by` operand of a satisfy usage is a feature reference or feature chain (SysML.xtext `SatisfactionReferenceExpression`), and one written as a wider expression (`satisfy requirement sr : R1 by sub#(1);`, `by (a, b)`, `by parts->first()`, `by self.items#(2)`) is now reported once, where the chain ends, with the usage closing at its `;` so the members after it parse undisturbed, instead of a cascade of three diagnostics about the declaration, a metadata prefix and a body member.
- A satisfaction declared with its requirement definition as type (`satisfy requirement sr : R1 by s;`) is checked against the definition's conditions (SysML v2 §7.20.5, a `SatisfyRequirementUsage` is a `RequirementUsage`): `-satisfy`, `%satisfy` and `VerifySatisfaction` decide it as they decide `satisfy r by s;`, where they refused it with `no requirement to satisfy`; one whose definition states no condition reports `no condition to evaluate`.
- Reading `result` on a satisfy usage (`touchdown.result` over `satisfy requirement touchdown : Touchdown by gentle;`) evaluates the requirement's conditions with every subject it declares bound to the object `by` names, as checking the satisfaction does (`Requirements.sysml` `RequirementCheck::result`), instead of `<undetermined>` for the unbound subject.
