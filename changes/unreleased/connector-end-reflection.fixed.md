- **Reflect connector ends and feature-chain targets.** A connection usage's `connect` clause now owns the ends it writes, named or not, so `ownedMember`, `ownedElement`, `ownedFeature`, `ownedEndFeature` and `endFeature` of its metaobject hold the two end `ReferenceUsage`s, and the API JSON export lists them under `ownedMember` as it did under `connectorEnd`. Each end owns the `ReferenceSubsetting` its attachment states, so `ownedReferenceSubsetting` and `ownedSubsetting` of an end derive; they used to fail with `reflective feature is not derived`. A relationship target written as a feature chain (`connect s.y to k.u`, `attribute :>> sub.w`) is now reflected as the feature the chain denotes, owned by the relationship as `ownedRelatedElement` and answering its `chainingFeature` in order, for a loaded model and a library record alike; its `target`, `subsettedFeature`, `redefinedFeature` and `referencedFeature` used to be underived. A usage of an interface whose definition pairs its ends by a `flow` no longer warns that the ends are not conjugate, and a flow is read by the end its chain starts at, so two ends typed by the same port are paired only by flows between them, not by a flow within one end.
