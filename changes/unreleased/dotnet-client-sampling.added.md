- The .NET client runs behaviors repeatedly, self-checks models and records runs. `Model.RunActionRunsAsync` runs an action a number of times with its inputs, a performer, the features to observe, a seed, a draw policy, a clock step and an engine, and `RunAnalysisRunsAsync` a `Simulation::MonteCarlo` analysis case with its subject and arguments; both answer `Runs`, one row per run with each observable's `RunDistribution` (extremes, mean, deviation, percentiles and histogram, or counts of distinct values), the lines `sysml -runs` prints, the seed echoed, and for a case its conclusion over the sample. `SelfCheckAsync` applies the `SysMLValidation` constraints, and those of any rule packages, to every element, answering a `SelfCheck` with every finding and count. `RecordRunAsync` runs an analysis case once, over a sweep or as a Monte Carlo sample and records the runs into the model, answering a `Recording` with the runs, the records and their source, the documents they were merged into and the recorded model as a new `Model`, leaving the original as it was and writing nothing to disk. A failed run stays a row; runs the service could not table raise `RunsException` keeping the table made, and a recording not made raises `RecordException` keeping the runs and whether they reported the failure. The conformance runner covers these three RPCs, and its comparison with the reference runner leaves out the wall time each row of runs reports and the time a recording states in its records, comparing the recorded model's hash only as present.
