- The interconnection rendering now draws the ports a part has from its definition — `part heating : HeatingSystem` shows the `durationIn : ~DurationPort` its definition declares — on the part's border, named and typed as declared with `~` for a conjugated one, and ends a connector, interface, flow or binding at the port it names rather than at the part: DOT as record cells or pin nodes (`"n2":"n2.0" -> "n1":"n1.0"`), PlantUML as `port` elements of the part's rectangle, text as `port` lines under the part and `part.port` edge ends, Mermaid as a `subgraph` per ported part holding a node per pin (`n1.0["durationIn"]`) with the connector drawn pin to pin. By default a part draws the ports a connector of the view ends at and no other — each a small square on the part's border with its name beside it, so a crowded diagram shows what it connects and nothing more, a part none of whose ports is connected drawing as before — and `-render-ports full` (`%render … full`, `"ports": "full"` on `opensysml/render`, `Diagram::ports` in a document) draws every port a part has, labelled `name : Type`; the new `openSysmlRenderPorts` capability lists the two displays. Previously only a part def's own nested ports were drawn, so `-render '#interconnection:Toaster'` and a declared interconnection view named no port at all. A part whose definition declares no port draws as before. `-render -help` now states the `#<kind>` and `#<kind>:<element>` pseudo-view forms. A Mermaid flowchart's multi-line subgraph titles are centred line under line by a `themeCSS` rule in the frontmatter that reserves their height, instead of being set flush left inside the centred block. Every graphical form now heads a node with its kind, `«part def»`, above the name and centred, as the graphical notation and Cameo stack a header; the kind stood under the name before.
